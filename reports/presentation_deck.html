⚙️ 4-Stage Probabilistic Machine Learning Pipeline
No Data Leakage • Chronological Splits

Rather than predicting subjective rankings directly, our pipeline reconstructs the delivery physics, calculates situation expectations, prices margins into run currency, and shrinks estimates with Bayesian partial pooling.

Stage 1 • Model A
Situation Baselines

Calibrated Gradient-Boosted Trees (HistGradientBoosting) estimate expected runs, dismissal probability, and bowler conceded runs based on continuous balls remaining, wickets in hand, and RRR.

Stage 2 • Model B
Leverage Spread

Outcome volatility across potential transitions o ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 6, wicket}:
L(s) = sqrt(∑ P(o|s)[p(s_o) - p_bar]²) × urgency. Top 20% identifies high pressure.

Stage 3
Run-Equivalent Value

Synthesizes runs, boundaries, dots, and wickets into unified run-equivalent margins with situational wicket values c(s_b):
V_bat = r_runs + c(s) · r_survival

Stage 4 • Model C
Hierarchical Bayes

Decomposes ability into population priors, normal baseline (a_i), and pressure uplift (d_i). Ranks by 10th percentile conservative score (Q0.10).

🛡️ The Baseline Penalty Solution

Simple subtraction (Pressure SR − Overall SR) unfairly penalizes superstars like Jasprit Bumrah or Suryakumar Yadav because their overall level is already so high that their "lift" seems smaller than average players.

Model C solves this via additive random effects:
V_b = μ + a_i + (β + d_i) H_b + ε_b
A player receives credit for both their elite foundation (a_i) and their situational uplift (d_i).

📉 Conservative Q0.10 Scoring Logic

Instead of sorting by posterior mean, we take the 10th percentile:
Score_i = Q_0.10(Pressure Ability Posterior)

The Mathematical Guarantee: If a player has a small sample size (e.g., 40 balls), their posterior distribution is wide, pushing their 10th percentile down. Only players with large sample sizes and sustained excellence maintain a high Q0.10 score.
