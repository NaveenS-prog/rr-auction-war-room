⚙️ The 4-Stage Quantitative Evaluation Framework
Zero Data Leakage • Chronological Validation

Rather than assigning subjective clutch tags, our system reconstructs legal delivery physics, conditions continuously on match context, converts performance into run-currency, and calibrates uncertainty via Bayesian partial pooling.

Stage 1
Match Context Baselines

Calibrated gradient-boosted trees predict expected runs, dismissal probability, and conceded bowling economy based on balls remaining, wickets in hand, and target run rates.

Stage 2
Leverage Volatility

Dynamic win-probability outcome volatility across legal delivery transitions:
L(s) = sqrt(∑ P(o|s)[p(s_o) - p_bar]²) × urgency. Top 20% identifies true pressure.

Stage 3
Run-Equivalent Currency

Synthesizes runs, boundaries, dots, and wickets into unified run-equivalent margins with situational wicket valuations c(s_b):
V_bat = r_runs + c(s) · r_survival

Stage 4
Bayesian Calibration

Decomposes ability into baseline skill (a_i) and pressure uplift (d_i). Ranks by 10th percentile conservative score (Q0.10) to strictly penalize variance.

🛡️ The Baseline Penalty Solution

Simple subtraction (Pressure Strike Rate − Overall Strike Rate) unfairly penalizes superstars like Jasprit Bumrah or Suryakumar Yadav because their overall level is already so high that their "lift" seems smaller than average players.

The Bayesian Reliability Engine solves this via additive random effects:
V_b = μ + a_i + (β + d_i) H_b + ε_b
A player receives full credit for both their elite foundation (a_i) and their situational uplift (d_i).

📉 Conservative Q0.10 Scoring Logic

Instead of sorting by posterior mean, we order players by the 10th percentile:
Score_i = Q_0.10(Pressure Ability Posterior)

The Mathematical Guarantee: If a player has a small sample size, their posterior distribution is wide, pushing their 10th percentile down. Only players with large sample sizes and sustained excellence maintain a high Q0.10 score.
